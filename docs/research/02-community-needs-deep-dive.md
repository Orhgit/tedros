# Community Needs — Deep Dive

**Date**: 2026-04-27
**Author**: Vega (research synthesis from 12 sources, see end)
**Status**: Phase 0.5 — input to Vision v2.

A structured map of what an Ethiopian-Israeli community member actually needs across the life journey, grounded in published statistics, government audits, and the ecosystem of existing service organizations. This document supersedes the narrow "real-estate first" framing of the original Phase 0 summary by widening the scope to **every domain a community member touches** — while keeping real-estate as the primary monetization vehicle so the broader portal is sustainable.

## Population baseline

- **177,600** people (CBS, end of 2024) — ~1.76% of Israel's population. ([CBS]) ([WP-EJI])
- **53%** Ethiopia-born; **47%** Israel-born (second generation).
- **Geographic concentration**: Center 39% (Netanya 12.2K leads), South 24%; Kiryat Malakhi has the highest community share at 15.1%.
- **Local-authority representation**: <1.4% of municipal employees nationwide despite 2% population share. ([State Comptroller 2024])

## Cross-cutting findings

### Education gap

- High-school diploma eligibility: **36.8%** community vs **50.8%** general Jewish population — a 14-point gap, despite near-equal exam-taking rates (70.8% vs 71%). ([Knesset MMM])
- **10%** community youth haven't completed 12 years of schooling (vs 8.5% general). ([Knesset MMM])
- Rising school dropout, juvenile delinquency, depression, suicide cases since late 1990s. ([Humanium])

### Welfare load

- **41%** of community youth registered with welfare services (vs ~14% general).
- **22%** face poverty; **61%** face poor parental/child functioning. ([Knesset MMM])

### Employment & income

- Substantially lower wages and higher unemployment vs general Jewish population.
- Concentrated in low-wage sectors with limited advancement.
- Despite 2008 government decision and follow-up plans, public-sector senior representation remains minimal. ([JCPA], [Adva Center])
- **Olim Beyahad** has placed >700 academics with 90% retention; this is the _exception_ that proves the systemic problem.

### Mental health

- Higher psychiatric hospitalization rates than general population. ([Springer Nature])
- Severe shortage of Amharic-fluent providers compounds stigma. ([NIH PMC])
- **Practical gap**: no public directory of Amharic-speaking therapists, social workers, or psychiatrists.

### Domestic violence

- Complaint rate significantly higher than population share. Murders by spouse spiked 2001–2006.
- Cultural transition disrupts traditional family-role structure → spousal tension. ([BIU Gender Studies], [Nogafem])
- Community-led empowerment networks (recognized 2022 Facebook community of the year) exist but operate informally.

### Law enforcement

- **3.5%** of police cases opened against community members (2019–2022) vs **1.7%** population share — exactly **2x** the rate. ([Calcalist])
- Complaints to מח"ש (Police Internal Investigations) at **4.5%** of all complaints (2017–2020) — **2.6x** population share. ([State Comptroller 7302])
- No body consolidates community police-conduct complaints; many are closed for "complainant abandonment."

### Real estate (existing pillar)

- **Mortgage 600K, 0% for 10 years**: 800 families/cycle eligible; lottery enrollment 70 NIS at participating bank branches. Active. ([Ynet]) Verified in original discovery doc.
- **Urban renewal**: 5 priority neighborhoods with cultural-bridge mandate (Rehovot, Rishon, Netanya).
- **Aliyah housing 2026**: First "direct absorption" pilot — Falash Mura olim placed directly into housing rather than klita centers. ([Ynet 2026])

### Heritage, religion, culture

- **Sigd** — central community holiday, 29 Cheshvan (Beta Israel covenant renewal); recognized state holiday.
- **Kessim** state-recognized 2018 as religious leadership; two yeshivas (Ashkelon, Beersheba). ([Hidabroot])
- **Center for Heritage** preserving oral → written tradition; primary academic node. ([ethiopianjhc.org.il])
- **Falash Mura aliyah**: govt did NOT fund continuation in 2025 budget — political risk for new immigrants. ([Knesset 03/2025])

## Strategic implications

### A. The thesis

Tedros's competitive position is **the Action Layer for an Information Ecosystem**.

- Information already exists across Kol-Zchut, ENP, Tebeka, Tene Briut, govt sites.
- Trust already lives at Tene Briut, Tebeka, ENP.
- What is missing: the unified surface where reading turns into doing — applying, booking, listing, complaining, finding a person — in HE/EN/AM.

### B. Three-layer product model

Every pillar exposes the same three layers:

1. **Read** — Programmatic SEO content, multi-lingual, sourced. Wins ranking by being more useful per page than Kol-Zchut's encyclopedia format.
2. **Do** — Wizards, applications, booking, complaint-filing, status-checking. Wins users by saving them the _Hebrew-bureaucracy-tax_ that the community pays disproportionately.
3. **Connect** — Vetted professionals, mentors, peer support, kessim, klita centers. Wins trust by curating community-from-within.

### C. Why the existing real-estate-first framing is too narrow

- Real estate alone won't acquire users in 90 days. SEO authority on real-estate is contested (Yad2, Madlan, Komo) and slow.
- Information pillars (rights, scholarships, programs) acquire users **faster** because the SEO competition is weaker (Kol-Zchut is encyclopedic; we are action-driven).
- A wider portal _funds_ itself through real-estate leads (existing plan) AND professional referrals AND optional employer postings — a portfolio of income streams instead of a single-track risk (R8: lead model unvalidated).

### D. Three "trust anchors" to land before public launch

- **Tene Briut** — owns community-trusted health voice. Lock in Amharic-provider-directory content collaboration.
- **Tebeka** — owns community-trusted legal voice. Lock in complaint-filing routing partnership.
- **ENP or Olim Beyahad** — owns community-trusted education/employment voice. Co-branded scholarship + job feeds.

Without at least Tene Briut and Tebeka in place, any health/legal content we publish risks looking like another well-meaning generalist site.

## Open questions

These move into the interview protocol (now needing expansion beyond real estate):

1. Which life-journey stage is most _underserved_ today, in the user's lived experience? (Hypothesis: information-to-action gap on rights claims.)
2. For mental health, would the user prefer an _Amharic-fluent_ provider or a _culturally fluent_ one? (Could be different.)
3. Is there appetite for an anonymous racism/police-conduct reporting flow on Tedros, or does the user prefer Tedros stay neutral and direct them to Tebeka?
4. What language preference dominates _navigation_ vs _content_? (Hypothesis: HE-first navigation, AM-first content for elders, EN-first content for diaspora visitors.)
